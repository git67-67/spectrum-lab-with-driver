Resolution and output

The original ArgyllCMS values are always kept. Every measurement stores the 10 nm bands and the 3.33 nm high-resolution values computed from the same pixels (the factory 36-band output and the ArgyllCMS high-resolution grid), plus the 128 raw pixel values, whatever resolution or kernel you display or export. The 128 pixels are not on an even wavelength grid and each one responds to a range of wavelengths; the raw pixels are the only true measured data.

Live recording and averaging

Live keeps measuring with the exposure it found, shows every update and the running average, and plots brightness, CCT, Duv, CRI, TM-30 … over time (card on the left). A short reading time updates faster but is noisier; the rolling mean brings the noise back down.

Fixed number of readings for the Measure button (1 = a single reading; the mean goes into the table).

Calibrate against a known source (blackbody)

Measure a lamp whose spectrum you know (a tungsten halogen or incandescent lamp at a known colour temperature). The page derives a per-band multiplier so the instrument shows that source's shape, and applies it to every following light measurement while the switch "Source (blackbody) correction" is on. It does not change the absolute level.

Calibrate the absolute level (brightness / power)

The factory scale is assumed to be mW/m²/nm and has not been checked against a reference. Here you can scale every following light measurement so that a source of known level reads correctly. This is one factor for the whole spectrum (the spectral shape is handled by the blackbody correction above). Measure the reference with the same exposure settings and geometry you will use later.

Blackbody at several temperatures (find the true temperature)

Use a lamp whose temperature you can change (supply voltage or current) and whose true temperature you do not know exactly. Set a temperature, press Add this temperature, change the lamp, repeat (at least 3 steps, as far apart as you can). Steps in temperature are determined from the data alone, whatever the instrument's colour response or the filament's emissivity. The absolute temperature needs one more fact: the true temperature of one step (pyrometer, or the filament resistance of a tungsten lamp). Without it the factory colour calibration and a grey emitter are trusted.

Calibration data (EEPROM)

The factory calibration lives in the instrument's EEPROM and is read from it when you connect. Back it up, or load another one (a .bin, a hex dump, or the "-D9" output of spotread). A loaded file replaces the wavelength matrix, linearisation and white tile data used here; the dark and white calibrations must then be redone. Nothing is written to the instrument.

Readout and display

Brightness and power are computed from the factory-calibrated spectrum, which the instrument reports in mW/m²/nm. Luminance = 683 lm/W × the Y-weighted integral, so it is in cd/m² for a light that fills the instrument's view and lux behind a diffuser. The calibration factor lets you match a trusted meter: set it to (their value) ÷ (value shown here). Not yet checked against a reference meter for every unit. The calibration factor applies to measurements from now on.

Instrument info and diagnostics

If you see "Instrument did not answer (timeout)": the page resets the instrument and retries once by itself. If it keeps happening, plug the i1 Pro directly into the computer (not a hub), close other programs that use it, keep this tab in the foreground, and send the diagnostic log with your report.
